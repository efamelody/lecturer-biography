Academic Leadership, Public Outreach, and Media Engagement Portfolio: Professor Dr. Mohd Talib Latif
Executive Profile and Scholarly Footprint
Professor Dr. Mohd Talib Latif is an atmospheric chemist and environmental scientist whose research centers on atmospheric aerosols, surface ozone dynamics, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and transboundary air pollution across Southeast Asia1. He holds a Doctorate in Atmospheric Chemistry from the University of East Anglia4 and serves as a Professor in the Department of Earth Sciences and Environment at Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia (UKM)1. Effective August 1, 2026, he was appointed Director of the Institute for Climate Change (IPI UKM)6.
His professional honors include election as a Fellow of the Academy of Sciences Malaysia (ASM)3, Chairman of the ASM Climate Change Committee7, and President of the Malaysian Association for Aerosol and Air Quality Research (MAAQR)8. Globally, he serves on the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) Task Force Bureau (TFB)6 and maintains leadership roles in the International Global Atmospheric Chemistry – Monsoon Asia and Oceania Geoscience Initiative (IGAC-MANGO)3. Recognized among the world's top 2% of scientists11 and recipient of the 2018 Top Research Scientists Malaysia (TRSM) award from the Academy of Sciences Malaysia12, his scientific work links chemical measurements of particulate matter ( and )1 with national environmental policy, public health protection, and regional climate adaptation strategies3. His primary scholarly metrics are indexed on his ResearchGate Profile and personal website talib-latif.com1.

National Press and International Media Coverage
Prof. Dr. Mohd Talib Latif frequently provides media commentary across major national and international press outlets, converting complex atmospheric chemistry concepts into accessible public knowledge9. His public communication focuses on four core domains: scientific evaluation of air quality indices, mitigation of transboundary biomass haze, urban microclimate heat dynamics, and transboundary volcanic sulfur dispersal15.
Technical Clarification of Air Quality Metrics
In October 2026, amid widespread public debate regarding conflicting air pollution readings in Kuala Lumpur, Prof. Latif provided technical clarity in the New Straits Times and Harian Metro regarding the operational differences between international commercial aggregators, such as IQAir, and the official Air Pollutant Index Management System (APIMS) operated by Malaysia's Department of Environment (DOE)18. He explained that while third-party platforms rely on satellite estimates and varied threshold categories—which can occasionally misinterpret atmospheric water vapor or cloud cover as elevated particulate matter—Malaysia’s official Air Pollutant Index (API) relies on 68 continuous ground-level monitoring stations nationwide compliant with USEPA and ISO standards18. He advised the public to rely primarily on official national API readings for localized health actions while reserving international index platforms for broader cross-country comparisons18.
Transboundary Haze, Peatland Agriculture, and Open Burning
Prof. Latif provides expert analysis on regional haze episodes affecting Southeast Asia14. In media interviews with Sinar Harian, Malay Mail, Astro Awani, and Harian Metro, he outlined how transboundary haze is driven by a combination of monsoonal wind patterns, ambient temperatures, and humidity levels interacting with peatland fires during the dry season14. To combat recurrent open burning, he advocates for agricultural biochar technology and controlled composting, which transform agricultural crop residues into soil conditioners rather than airborne smoke14. Furthermore, he consistently notes that local urban emissions, such as vehicular exhaust and localized rubbish burning, compound transboundary haze, requiring local municipal enforcement alongside regional ASEAN commitments20.
Urban Microclimates, Heat Islands, and Traffic Density
In analyses reported by Free Malaysia Today and Astro Awani, Prof. Latif examined the relationship between urban vehicular congestion, microclimatic heat accumulation, and ground-level ozone () synthesis17. He demonstrated that dense vehicular queues accelerate volatile organic compound (VOC) buildup, worsening the urban heat island (UHI) effect across major metropolitan centers like the Klang Valley6. His scientific evaluations during the Movement Control Order (MCO) reported by Astro Awani revealed measurable drops in fine particulate matter () and carbon monoxide (CO), illustrating the direct baseline influence of vehicular traffic on urban air quality13.
Volcanic Aerosol Dynamics and Transboundary Sulfur
Following the April 2024 eruption of Mount Ruang in Indonesia, Prof. Latif provided scientific analysis in Utusan Malaysia on the atmospheric transport of high-altitude sulfur dioxide () plumes16. He detailed how volcanic sulfate aerosols disperse across regional airspace, presenting distinct respiratory hazards and acid deposition risks compared to typical organic biomass haze16.

Academic Conferences, Keynote Speeches, and Scientific Leadership
Prof. Dr. Mohd Talib Latif actively leads international scientific symposia, delivers keynote addresses, and chairs expert panels dedicated to continuous emissions monitoring, aerosol physics, and atmospheric policy3.
Continuous Emissions Monitoring (CEM) & AQUAMATE Asia 2026
As Head of the Scientific Committee for CEM & AQUAMATE Asia 2026, Prof. Latif established the scientific agenda addressing industrial continuous emissions monitoring, air quality sensor networks, and industrial decarbonization strategies across Asian markets3. He delivered a keynote presentation featured in the CEM Asia Programme entitled "Current Understanding and Future Direction of Ground-Level Air Quality Measurements in Malaysia," detailing national air monitoring frameworks, fine particulate chemical composition, and the integration of advanced analytical chemistry tools into policy enforcement11.
International Global Atmospheric Chemistry (iCACGP-IGAC) Conference
Prof. Latif served on the executive organizing and scientific committees for the joint iCACGP-IGAC Conference26. Through his leadership within the IGAC-MANGO regional working group, he facilitated scientific collaborations across South and Southeast Asia, focusing on biomass burning fingerprints, ground-level ozone formation, and sea-surface microlayer exchanges1.
Air Quality & Climate Change Conference (AQCC) & MAQAS
Prof. Latif co-organized and chaired the 1st International Conference of Air Quality & Climate Change in conjunction with the 3rd Malaysia Air Quality Annual Symposium on the 1st AQCC & 3rd MAQAS Portal9. Organized under the theme "Combating Climate Change and Improving Air Quality in a Global Pandemic," the conference brought together international experts to address pandemic emission shifts, atmospheric chemistry, and regional mitigation policy9.
Surface Ocean – Lower Atmosphere Study (SOLAS) Southeast Asia
In regional oceanographic and atmospheric research, Prof. Latif served as a featured panelist for the SOLAS Southeast Asia Workshop on YouTube29. His contributions addressed air-sea aerosol interactions, biogenic halocarbon emissions, and the sea-to-air flux of organic surfactants along the Malaysian coast1.

Broadcast Media, Digital Webinars, Policy Reports, and Community Outreach
In addition to academic publishing and press interviews, Prof. Latif leads major national policy reports, executive leadership webinars, broadcast panel discussions, and community science programs6.
UNICEF & UKM Technical Report on Climate & Child Health
Prof. Latif served as a lead co-researcher on the UNICEF-UKM Full Technical Report PDF examining the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation on children in Malaysia32. The project synthesized environmental exposure data with national legislative reviews, conducting national stakeholder consultations with government ministries to formulate child-sensitive climate policies32.
Academic Leadership & Sabbatical Knowledge Sharing
Through the Higher Education Leadership Academy (AKEPT) and UKM webinar series referenced on IPI UKM News and his UKM CV, Prof. Latif regularly presents on academic leadership, sabbatical research output, and institutional environmental management6. These lectures provide frameworks for establishing competitive atmospheric laboratories, securing international grant funding, and managing multidisciplinary research teams7.
Broadcasts and Science Communication
Prof. Latif contributes to televised and streamed public awareness programming33. Notable broadcasts include the UKM Science Media production Fenomena Jerebu di Asia Tenggara: Punca dan Penyelesaian on YouTube, where he detailed the chemical constituents of biomass smoke, health risks linked to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and protective health measures during severe pollution episodes1. His radio commentaries are featured on Bernama Radio33.

Strategic Content Architecture for Personal Website Integration
To optimize these media assets for display on Prof. Dr. Mohd Talib Latif’s official website (talib-latif.com), the content can be organized into four distinct media modules8:
Module 1: News & Press Commentary Repository
Serves as an archiving space for press clippings, direct interview quotes, and hyperlinked media articles from major outlets, including New Straits Times, Malay Mail, Astro Awani, Sinar Harian, and Harian Metro13. It features a dedicated technical section highlighting his national air quality metric explanations (API vs. AQI) as a reference resource for visiting journalists18.
Module 2: Academic Conferences, Keynotes & Symposia
Houses a structured inventory of keynote addresses, conference session chair positions, and scientific committee appointments across international events such as CEM Asia 2026, iCACGP-IGAC 2024, and AQCC & MAQAS 20219. Includes downloadable presentation slide decks (PDF) and links to indexed proceedings in IOP Conference Series: Earth and Environmental Science1.
Module 3: Broadcasts, Podcasts & Digital Media
Contains embedded video streams of recorded public lectures, radio interview recordings (e.g., Bernama Radio Portal), and digital workshop panels (e.g., SOLAS Southeast Asia Workshop on YouTube, AKEPT-UKM Series)6. Each recording is paired with a brief summary outlining key takeaways, timestamps, and related academic publications1.
Module 4: Policy Technical Reports & Community Outreach
Provides full text access to major technical advisory reports, policy monographs, and institutional documents, including the UNICEF-UKM Climate Report PDF, IPCC Task Force Bureau contributions, and Academy of Sciences Malaysia Climate Change Committee outputs7. This section highlights the bridge between laboratory research and high-level environmental policy formulation3.
